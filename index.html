Governing equation

Uniform flow in the x direction through a homogeneous, confined aquifer of thickness b, with linear equilibrium sorption and first-order decay of the solute (in both dissolved and sorbed phases):

R ∂C∂t = −v ∂C∂x + DL ∂²C∂x² + DT ∂²C∂y² − λR C

v = q/φ is the pore (seepage) velocity, DL = αLv + Dm and DT = αTv + Dm. Sorption slows everything by R: the plume centre moves at u = v/R.

Instantaneous injection (pulse)

C = M4π φ R b t √(D′L D′T) exp[ − (x−x0−ut)²4D′L t − (y−y0)²4D′T t − λt ]

with D′ = D/R. Continuous and finite-duration injections are obtained by superposing pulses in time (convolution), evaluated with Gauss–Legendre quadrature in 2D and the closed-form erfc solution in 1D. Several sources add up linearly.

Assumptions to discuss in class

  • Infinite, homogeneous aquifer: no boundaries, no heterogeneity, constant q.
  • The injection is fully mixed over the aquifer thickness (2D, depth-averaged).
  • In 1D the mass is given per unit cross-sectional area of the column (kg/m²).
  • Dispersivities are constant; in real aquifers αL grows with travel distance (scale effect).
  • Sorption is linear and instantaneous: R = 1 + ρbKd/φ.
